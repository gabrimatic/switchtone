# Platform audit

What macOS 26 and 27 and Mac hardware offer, and what Switchtone does with each. **Adopted** means it ships. **Not adopted** gives the reason, so the decision is on record.

Measurements come from a MacBook Pro with macOS 27.2, a notch, a 120 Hz ProMotion display and the built-in speakers.

## Adopted

| Capability | How Switchtone uses it |
| --- | --- |
| **AVAudioEnvironmentNode (3D audio, headphones)** | On headphones every key is a point source at its place on a MacBook keyboard: x from its column, depth from its row, 30 cm below and 40 cm in front of the ears, the edge keys about 36 degrees out. Each voice is its own mono `AVAudioSourceNode`. The node is in the graph only on this path: idle, it costs 1.6% of a core. |
| **HRTFHQ on headphones** | The route is read from Core Audio (transport type, plus the jack's data source). Headphones and AirPods get `HRTFHQ` with `outputType = .headphones`. Measured on a real sample: the edge keys reach the near ear 290 µs earlier and 3.8 dB louder, and every key sits within 0.5 dB of its mirror key. |
| **Stereo stage on speakers** | One `AVAudioSourceNode` mixes every voice with its own equal-power gains and an inter-channel delay of up to 0.35 ms, near channel first. The middle of the stage is where the hands meet, so F and J, D and K, S and L, A and the semicolon are mirror images. Measured: far left +11.4 dB and +354 µs, Delete -11.0 dB and -354 µs, the right hand's J K L ; at -3.2, -4.7, -6.3 and -7.9 dB. Read from the real output buffers: the key left of 1 +11.9, S +6.6, F +3.3, J -3.1, L -6.1, Delete -11.2 dB. |
| **Head tracking (`listenerHeadTrackingEnabled`)** | With 3D on headphones, AirPods that support head tracking keep the keyboard in place when you turn your head. It can be switched off in the panel. |
| **Low-latency output** | A 64-frame output buffer (1.3 ms at 48 kHz) and voices read directly on the render thread, so a strike waits only for the next cycle. The whole measured path on the built-in speakers: tap callback about 1 ms after the key event (median, from the app's counters; 4 to 7 ms for the first key after a quiet spell), 0.7 ms to the audio cycle (median, 1.4 ms worst), 1.33 ms output buffer, 1.0 ms safety offset, then 15.6 ms of device and stream latency (60 + 690 frames at 48 kHz). That is about 20 ms from key to air, 3 ms of it Switchtone. The last 16.6 ms is the driver's: the same 15.6 to 15.8 ms at 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz, reported by Core Audio and not measured acoustically, because the microphone's own path is reported at 50 ms and needs a permission Switchtone does not ask for. The 3D graph adds nothing measurable: the stereo stage and 3D off both reach the cycle in the same time, and HRTF adds 0.02 ms. The hit itself starts within 2.5 ms of the sample's start. |
| **Lock-free event-tap path** | The tap thread reads settings from atomics (`Synchronization.Atomic`) and owns its typing state, the bookkeeping of the eight key voices and their random generator. A strike reaches the render thread through a single-producer ring (`StrikeRing`). The loaded bank is an atomically swapped pointer, released 2 s after a swap, and lookups go through a fixed table. Previews and a press that wakes the output play on six separate voices from the audio control queue, through their own ring. The tap thread takes no lock, never allocates, never calls the system random number generator, and never waits on the main thread or the audio hardware; on the HRTF path it also sets the voice's position. The render thread takes no lock and allocates nothing. |
| **Speed dynamics** | The rhythm of typing sets each strike continuously: a slow press is full level, bright and rings out over about 80 ms; at full speed a key is half as loud, darker and rings out in half the time. Strikes are independent voices that overlap, each making equal-power room for what is still ringing. |
| **Release timing** | A release plays when the spring has returned the keycap, 6 to 13 ms after the key-up event and never closer than 22 ms to its own press, and it gets quieter with speed. It is scheduled on the host clock, so it lands to the sample. A release with no press heard stays silent. |
| **Stabilizer rattle** | Space, Return, Delete and Shift get three short metallic ticks 6.5 to 22 ms after the hit, 10 to 21 dB under it, different for each sample. They are baked at load time and leave the hit itself untouched. |
| **Route changes** | Core Audio listeners on the default device and on the jack's data source re-pick the rendering live. The engine's configuration-change notification restarts output on the new device. |
| **Low Power Mode and thermal state** | Under Low Power Mode, or when the thermal state is serious or critical, headphones fall back from HRTFHQ to HRTF. |
| **Idle power** | The output pauses after one quiet minute (a 10 s check runs while the output runs, so the pause lands within 70 s); at volume 0, or while off, muted by a Focus or not allowed, it never starts: about 1 to 2% of a core while running and silent (the 64-frame IO cycle), 0.02% paused. Wake, unlock, a modifier or a key starts it again; so does a left click within two minutes of the last key, but never in Low Power Mode or under thermal pressure, and never a scroll (the tap does not receive scrolls). A press that wakes a paused output still plays if the output is ready within 120 ms (a restart measures 35 to 70 ms). While the output is paused there are no timers, apart from the permission and silent-tap watches below in their first two minutes. While Input Monitoring is missing, Switchtone checks on each app switch, every second while System Settings is in front, and every 20 s otherwise (measured: under 0.01 s of CPU in 60 s, 3 checks). |
| **SwiftUI Liquid Glass** | Glass means interactive (docs/DESIGN.md). The panel is an `NSPopover` with the flat ground over it. The three profile cards are the main controls and carry interactive glass (`glassEffect(.regular.interactive())`) inside one `GlassEffectContainer`, with the chosen card washed in the accent. Buttons use `.glass` and `.glassProminent`. Inner shapes are `ConcentricRectangle`s, concentric with the panel. |
| **SF Symbols** | Line symbols only, in the text color: rows, notices, the variable-value volume symbol and symbol replace transitions. The app icon and the menu bar symbol are one original drawing, never a symbol. |
| **ProMotion 120 Hz** | All motion is SwiftUI springs (`.snappy`) and blur-replace transitions, rendered at the display's own rate. |
| **Force Touch haptics** | The volume slider reaching 0% or 100% (`.levelChange`), choosing a profile, Option-clicking the menu bar symbol (`.generic`), and the permission arriving. The trackpad's own haptics setting applies. |
| **Trackpad tick (private framework)** | A tap per press. There is no public API for one this light, so `MultitouchSupport` is loaded with `dlopen` behind a capability check: every symbol resolves, a device exists, its actuator opens. Measured on an M5 MacBook Pro: `MTActuatorOpen` and `MTActuatorActuate` return 0, a bogus device id gives no actuator, and one actuation takes about 3.8 ms, so it runs on its own thread and the tap only raises a flag. Bursts fold into one. The actuator is closed after 30 quiet seconds. Failures are silent. |
| **Accessibility** | VoiceOver labels, values and hints on every control, profile cards as selectable buttons, and notices read as one element. Reduce Transparency uses solid card surfaces. Increase Contrast adds borders. Reduce Motion stops the hover growth and turns changes into cross-fades. Switchtone becomes the active app while the panel is open, so the standard controls take Full Keyboard Access, Escape closes the panel and Command-Q quits; the previous app gets the keyboard back on close. |
| **App Intents: Shortcuts and Siri** | Toggle Switchtone Sounds, Turn Switchtone Sounds Off and On (fixed states), Set Switchtone Switches (profile), and Set Switchtone Volume, with Siri phrases from App Shortcuts. Set Switchtone Sounds (on or off) is hidden from the Shortcuts library (`isDiscoverable = false`). The metadata is generated at build time from the compiler's constant values. |
| **Focus filters** | The Switchtone Focus filter mutes the sounds while a chosen Focus is on. The panel says so, and the sounds return when the Focus ends. |
| **Control Center and menu bar control** | A `ControlWidgetToggle` in a sandboxed WidgetKit extension shows and sets whether the sounds are on; the app writes the state to a file the extension may read (read-only sandbox exception) and reloads the control on every change. Its intent runs only in the app (`allowedExecutionTargets = .main`). |
| **AppKit windows and menus** | The welcome window (flat ground, transparent title bar), the Settings window (a SwiftUI sidebar and panes under one hosting controller) and the About window are real windows. Switchtone stays an accessory app with no Dock icon in any state; while one of its windows is in front, its standard menus show (Switchtone, Edit, Window, Help). Switchtone Help opens Settings > About. Opening the app again shows the welcome window until it is finished, and Settings after that (`applicationShouldHandleReopen`). A second process hands over to the running one (`SingleInstance`). |
| **ServiceManagement** | The login item, registered on first launch. Approval and error states are shown in the panel. |
| **Input Monitoring (CGEventTap)** | A listen-only tap on its own thread, hearing key presses, releases and modifiers plus a left click (counted as a signal to warm the output; position and target are never read). The grant check (`CGPreflightListenEventAccess`, about 10 ms per call) always runs off the main thread. The welcome window asks once, through the system prompt. After that the panel's notice opens the exact pane, and Switchtone starts within about a second of the grant without a relaunch. A switch macOS holds for a different signature is detected and explained: when the tap runs but hears nothing while you type (also right after launch), Switchtone relaunches once, and if it is still deaf the panel asks for the switch to be turned off and on. Revoking the permission is noticed on wake and when the panel opens. |
| **Unified logging** | The log carries counters only, under the subsystem `info.gabrimatic.switchtone`: keys heard and played, skipped repeats, and the median, 95th percentile, average and maximum delay from the key event to Switchtone, plus the first key after a quiet spell. They are written when the panel opens, when the output pauses, at quit, and on `SIGUSR1` (`kill -USR1 $(pgrep -x Switchtone)` writes a `counters:` line). `rendering: HRTFHQ for headphones` (or `stereo stage for builtInSpeakers`) shows the 3D path in use, and `audio engine started in` names the output's own delay. If keys are typed and the counters stay at zero while another app has secure keyboard entry on, Switchtone logs that once: macOS hides every key from every app then. No key is ever logged. |

## Not adopted, and why

| Capability | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| **CMHeadphoneMotionManager** | `listenerHeadTrackingEnabled` does the same inside the audio engine, with no motion permission and no update loop in Switchtone. |
| **Metal and SwiftUI shaders** | Nothing in the settings panel needs custom rendering. The system glass and springs cover it. |
| **Foundation Models, Writing Tools, Translation** | Switchtone has no text to reason about, and must never look at what is typed. |
| **Vision, ScreenCaptureKit** | Switchtone has nothing to see. Screen access would be a far broader grant than a sound app should hold. |
| **User notifications** | Every state shows in the panel and the menu bar symbol. A notification for "permission granted" would be noise. |
| **Touch ID (LocalAuthentication)** | Switchtone stores nothing sensitive. Password fields are already silent (Mute when typing passwords). |
| **WidgetKit desktop widgets** | A widget that only shows "on" adds nothing over the menu bar symbol and the control. |
| **Ambient light and display brightness** | No public API ties to sound. Loudness follows the system volume and Switchtone's own slider. |
| **Multiple displays, Spaces, full screen** | The panel is a standard popover attached to the menu bar symbol, so the system places it on the right display and Space. Apart from its windows, Switchtone draws nothing else on screen. |
| **The system's spatial speaker path (`.auto`, `.builtInSpeakers`)** | Measured: symmetric but narrow, about 4.5 dB at the edge keys and under 3 dB across the right hand, with no way to widen one side of the image or add an inter-channel delay. It also loads about 45 MB of data and keeps the environment node running. The stereo stage places every key exactly, for free. |
| **AVAudioPlayerNode for the voices** | Measured at 3.1 ms from the play call to the first sample, against 0.7 ms for a voice read on the render thread, and each play needs pre-rendered pitch and tone copies: 8 MB per mono bank. |
| **A shorter speaker path** | The 15.6 ms belongs to the driver and is the same at every sample rate. An app cannot go around it. |
| **Custom key sound packs, per-app rules** | Out of scope. |
